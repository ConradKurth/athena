# Athena UraGuard — Compliant Positioning & Claims (2026-08-05)

Master reference for **all** marketing copy — outreach emails **and** website. Supersedes the antibacterial/UTI positioning the brand was originally built on.

## Why this exists

FDA consultant (via Dr. Jacqueline Kurth, MD — study sponsor) flagged: **do not use the 96% bacterial-reduction stat** — it triggers a **medical-device designation**. Broader principle: any antibacterial-efficacy or UTI/infection claim makes UraGuard a *regulated medical device* requiring FDA clearance; making such a claim without it = misbranding. **Removing the number is not enough — the whole antibacterial/UTI wedge has to come out** (an unquantified "reduces bacteria / UTI protection" is still a device claim). Until the brand chooses a regulatory/clinical path, all consumer marketing stays in the claim-free lane below.

The underlying study (Microchem NG22330) is also thin on its own: **non-GLP, single replicate, no stats, tests the film layer not the finished product, PBS not urine, no clinical endpoint.** Even if a claim were allowed, it wouldn't robustly support one.

## Claims — BANLIST (never, stated OR implied, brand OR curated)

- "antibacterial / antimicrobial" as a consumer benefit
- "reduces bacteria / bacterial concentration"; the **96%**; the lab study in any consumer-facing copy
- "reduces / prevents UTIs", "UTI protection", "urinary health", "recurrent UTIs", "reduces infection risk"
- structure/function dodges: "supports urinary health", "for a healthy urinary tract"
- **implied** claims (FTC treats these = explicit): UTI stats/factoids placed beside the product; question-framing ("Worried about UTIs?"); reviews *curated* for infection mentions
- any MD/expert quoted asserting an efficacy outcome
- disease/skin-condition words tied to the product: "prevents rash / dermatitis / infection"

## Claims — ALLOWLIST (safe, high confidence)

- **Dryness / performance:** "keeps you drier", "stays dry", "extra-dry layer", "overnight + leak security", "heavy-use protection" (absorbency/performance, not health)
- **Form factor (the genuine novelty):** a thin liner worn **inside** any incontinence brief/underwear for extra dryness — a mechanical benefit that gives the liner a reason to exist with zero antibacterial claim
- **Comfort / discretion:** thin, discreet, breathable, tailored fits, sizes
- **Brand:** dignity, comfort, confidence, "incontinence without shame"
- **Trust:** "physician-founded" / "designed with a doctor" — a credibility signal, NOT an efficacy claim (the MD must not assert an outcome)
- Dryness-comfort re skin is OK ("stay dry and comfortable"); disease words are not

## Consultant-gated — HOLD until cleared (do not use yet)

- **Odor / freshness** claims — the standard compliant path for a treated textile (same tech, reframed as freshness like antimicrobial activewear). Requires the biocide to be **EPA-registered** for that use + treated-article-exemption qualification. **Verify before using even "odor-control."**
- **Handing the AATCC-100 study to B2B / clinical buyers** as data — lowest-risk home for the bacteria result, but even B2B use can establish FDA **"intended use."** Get sign-off.

## Positioning (claim-free wedge)

Category: **dignified, discreet incontinence care for women + caregivers, physician-founded.** The wedge is NOT clinical — it's **design + the inside-the-brief liner form factor (extra-dry / overnight security) + a dignity brand.** Closest playbook: **Attn:Grace** (built a real brand on dignity/clean/discretion with no infection claim).

## The clever, compliant introduction angles (the "ideas" to build on)

1. **Sell the form factor + dryness** — the liner as an extra dry / overnight-security layer. Product truth, no claim. *(high)*
2. **Physician-founded, not physician-claimed** — borrow Dr. Kurth's MD credibility for trust without an efficacy statement. *(high)*
3. **Dignity/design brand** — Attn:Grace lane; emotional wedge, fully compliant. *(high)*
4. **Gift, don't script** — send creators/clinicians product; let them form + voice their own take (#ad disclosed). Never hand them the health conclusion. *(moderate — the line is real)*
5. **B2B data hand-off** — give pros the raw study as data to evaluate; don't assert the marketing claim. *(moderate — intended-use risk, consultant-gated)*
6. **Community-first sampling** — show up helpfully in caregiver/menopause/incontinence communities; let the product's feel sell it. *(high)*
7. **Odor/freshness reframe** — *(consultant-gated, see above)*

**Traps to refuse** (look clever, are the fast lane back into the problem): implied claims, "supports urinary health", UTI factoids next to the product, curated infection testimonials, "for educational purposes" 96% on a page that also sells.

## Re-segmentation (the prospect list must change)

- **FITS the compliant pitch:** general women's-health / incontinence / caregiver creators; dignity/design audiences; B2B facilities & associations on **dryness / comfort / dignity / workflow**.
- **DOES NOT fit (park until claim path decided):** UTI/IC advocates (IC Network), urology/urogyn clinics (Female Urology of Nashville, Total Urology), recurrent-UTI creators (Susan Winograd's menopause-UTI video, arguably Sara Reardon's leak-bacteria angle) — these were chosen **for** the clinical wedge and don't fit a dignity/dryness pitch.
